---
title: How to tailor your CV to a job description
description: A repeatable five-step method for matching your CV to a specific job posting, without inventing experience or rewriting the whole document each time.
publishedAt: "2026-11-16"
---

Sending the same CV to every job is the fastest way to apply and one of the slowest ways to get
an interview. A recruiter who opens your CV is checking one thing first: does this person match
what we asked for? Tailoring is how you make that answer obvious.

It does not mean rewriting your history for each application. It means choosing which true things
to put first. Here is a method you can repeat for every posting in about twenty minutes.

## 1. Read the posting for requirements, not for tone

Go through the job description and pull out the concrete requirements: skills, tools, years of
experience, responsibilities, and anything marked as required rather than preferred. Ignore the
company boilerplate.

Write them down as a plain list. Most postings come down to eight to twelve items, and three or
four of those matter far more than the rest. They are usually the ones that appear in the title,
in the first paragraph, and again in the requirements section.

## 2. Mark what you can prove

For each requirement, find the place in your experience that proves it. Be strict with yourself:

- **Direct match:** you have done exactly this, and can name where.
- **Adjacent match:** you have done something close, and can explain the overlap.
- **Gap:** you have not done it.

Gaps are normal. Postings describe an ideal candidate, and people are hired with gaps all the
time. What matters is that your direct matches are impossible to miss.

## 3. Reorder before you rewrite

Most of tailoring is ordering. Before changing a single sentence:

- Move the most relevant role details and bullet points to the top of each section.
- Put the skills the posting names first in your skills list.
- Cut or shorten bullets that have nothing to do with this job.

A CV that leads with the three things the employer asked for reads as a strong match, even when
every word in it is the same as in your general version.

## 4. Use the employer's words for the same thing

If the posting says "stakeholder management" and your CV says "worked with other teams", you are
describing the same work in words that are harder to recognize. Use the posting's term where it
is accurate.

This helps people skimming your CV, and it helps applicant tracking systems that search CVs for
specific terms. The limit is honesty: only use a term for something you have really done. Copying
keywords you cannot back up fails at the interview, if not before.

## 5. Rewrite the summary last

Your summary is the only part worth rewriting from scratch each time. In two or three sentences,
say who you are in the terms of this role and name your strongest direct matches. Write it last,
when you already know which matches you are leading with.

## Keep a base CV and tailor copies

The method only stays fast if you start from the same place every time. Keep one complete base
CV with everything you might want to use, and make a tailored copy for each application. Save
each copy with the application it went to, so that when a recruiter calls three weeks later you
know exactly which version they are looking at.
